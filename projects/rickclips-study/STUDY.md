measured at the native 30 fps (brightness, contrast, shadows, highlights, saturation, warm/cool
balance, motion), every shot and transition was detected, and every caption was read frame by frame
to rebuild the scripts. Frame sheets of every shot were reviewed by eye.

Limits: the files are YouTube's video-only streams, so there is **no audio** (no voice, music or

## The script formula

**1. Hook, first sentence, about 3 seconds.** A strange situation stated as plain fact, which forces a question:
- "The fastest gun in the West had one rule: never draw first."
- "He sold his happiest memory to a stranger behind a counter."
- "They buried a man alive with a bell tied to his finger."
- "He scanned a customer's lottery ticket, saw it had won a million dollars, and said, 'Sorry, man. Not a winner.'"
- Or a direct challenge: "Look at these two scratchcards. One's a winner and one's garbage. Yet there's a way to tell without scratching either of them."

**2. "You see," then the rule of the world.** His signature pivot (5 times in 4 of the 5 scripts). It sets up
the context in 1-3 sentences with concrete, slightly funny examples ("landlords take rent in birthdays",
"graves came with a little bell on top, tied by string to the body's finger").

**3. A character and an inciting event, told in present tense** ("he gets suspicious", "So he waits").
Characters get one name at most (Eddie, Jerry); usually it's just "a guy", "the cashier", "his wife".

**4. Escalation with specific numbers and comic exaggeration.**
- "three states away", "300 times without leaving his couch", "2 hours", "5 minutes go by. Then ten.", "90%"
- "He'd lose a race to the saloon against a guy carrying two beers."
- "So he does what any sane person would do. He blows his entire severance check..."

**5. The turn, on "But...", "Except...", "So what does he do?" or "Wait. Practiced?"** The narrator asks
the question the viewer is thinking, sometimes interrupting himself.

**6. The twist, then (for true stories) the real explanation:** "You see, in real life, lotteries noticed
that store clerks were winning way more often than everyone else..."

**7. The last line reframes everything, then the video ends.** No call to action, no outro.
- "He sold every happy year he ever had, and his brother was in every single one."
- "And the house was all hers."
- "By the end of the week, the game is gone from every store."

**Voice and rhythm.**
- Casual and conversational: "five bucks", "a normal person", "Story of my life".
- Second person when it helps immersion: "Imagine you're standing in the street at noon...".
- 12-18 words per sentence, almost no pauses, read fast (about 210-235 words per minute).
- Dialogue is quoted in short, deadpan lines.

**Story sources.** Three of the five are real facts dressed as stories:
- why reacting beats drawing first (Slow Eddie)
- the man who cracked a scratch-card game (Mohan Srivastava, Ontario, 2003)
- lottery stings against store clerks

The other two are a historical object (safety coffins) and one original parable (the memory shop).
So the formula is: an interesting true fact, told as a tiny story with a twist.

## The look

**Characters.**
- A round-headed cartoon cast: big cream-white circle head, minimal black-line face (dot or oval eyes,
  thick expressive brows, small mouth, toothy grins for scheming).
- Bean-shaped coloured body (the outfit), thin black stick limbs, small round hands.
- One strong identifying shape each: orange spiky hair, a bob, a curly moustache, a cowboy or bucket hat,
  a name badge reading KYLE.
- Bold black outlines. Characters stay flat-shaded but take on the scene's coloured light: blue faces at
  night, amber in lamplight, glowing when an object lights them.

**Environments.**
- Rich, detailed, 3D-looking sets: a Western street, a saloon, a telegraph office, a rainy city street,
  a hospital, a convenience store, a lottery office, a snowy Victorian town, a dock at sunset.
- Props carry readable custom text that tells the story: "Vault BUSTER WIN UP TO $500,000",
  "MEMORIES bought & sold", "WE BUY SUMMERS", a ledger "YEAR 2025 SOLD", "OUR WINNERS", "BAIT · GAS",
  a death certificate.

**Lighting.**
- Cinematic and always motivated by a source in the scene: desk lamps, hanging pendants, windows,
  candles, neon, the sun.
- Warm practical lights against cool blue night are the house look.
- Golden hour with long blue-violet shadows outdoors; bright, saturated midday for happy flashbacks.
- Rim light separates characters from the background.
- Volumetric light shafts through windows and blinds, floating dust motes, bloom on bright sources,
  glowing objects that light a face.

**Contrast and shadow.**
- Mid-dark exposure (average luma 0.36-0.52) with strong contrast.
- 9-27% of every frame sits in near-black shadow and only 3-12% in highlights, so it reads moody and rich.
- Soft contact shadows under characters, long cast shadows at sunset, blind-stripe shadow patterns,
  darkened frame edges, and a hooded shopkeeper who is a silhouette with glowing eyes.

**Colour.**
- Saturated: mean saturation around 0.5.
- Each video alternates warm and cool scenes to carry the mood. The sad memory-shop story is 73% cool;
  the scratch-card caper is mostly warm.
- Happy memories and fantasies are warm and sunlit; danger and loss are blue.

**Camera and movement.**
- The camera is almost never still: only 7-14% of frames have no motion.
- Slow push-ins on faces, dolly and truck moves through sets, orbits, tilts and crane moves.
- Low angles, Dutch tilts for tension, extreme close-ups of key props.
- Overhead shots of desks, maps and grids. POV shots through objects (from inside a trash can, through
  a porthole).
- Shallow depth of field with blurred foregrounds and backgrounds.
- Action beats get fast whip-tilts and crash zooms with motion blur.

**Editing.**
- 17-35 shots per video, averaging 1.8-3.4 s: a new picture for almost every clause.
- Each shot illustrates the words literally ("landlords take rent in birthdays" shows a landlord taking
  a birthday).
- Transitions are mostly soft cross-dissolves, plus hard cuts and camera-move transitions: zooming
  through a window into the next scene, or whipping down to a holster and back.

**Effects.**
- Muzzle flash, a slow-motion bullet with a fire trail, sparks and star glints, shockwave rings,
  motion-blur streaks.
- Dust, rain, snow, floating motes, glows, white flash transitions.
- Cartoon sweat drops, coins, and numbers lighting up on screens and cards.

**Captions.**
- 1 word at a time (70-75%), 2 words (20-27%), rarely 3.
- Each caption is on screen about 0.3 s and switches exactly on the spoken word, with no gaps and no
  animation.
- Heavy rounded sans-serif (close to Nunito Black / Montserrat ExtraBold), white fill, thin black
  outline, soft drop shadow.
- About 45 px tall on a 720 px-wide frame, centred horizontally, at 70% of the height (y = 900 of
  1280). Punctuation is kept.

## How it's made (my read)

The look is not hand-drawn frame by frame. The signs are the polished 3D-style render, the readable
generated text on props, each shot being a few seconds of smooth camera motion, and soft dissolves
between clips. It matches **AI image generation for the keyframes** (consistent characters, cinematic
lighting, text on props) **plus image-to-video for the motion** (5-10 s clips with a prompted camera
move). The clips are then cut to an AI voiceover and get word-by-word captions.

## How to replicate the style (with your own stories and characters)

1. **Script:** follow the 7-step formula above. Aim for 200-230 words (55-65 s), a true fact or an
   original twist, and "You see," as the pivot.
2. **Character sheet:** design your own cast in this round-head style (for example a new Clyde) and
   keep the sheet as the reference for every image.
3. **One keyframe per clause** (about 25-35 images), in Dreamina, ChatGPT or Gemini, with a style block like:
   > Vertical 9:16 cinematic still from a polished 3D-rendered cartoon short. Simple cartoon characters
   > with big round cream-white heads, minimal black-line faces, bean-shaped coloured bodies and thin
   > black stick limbs, bold black outlines. [SCENE]. Detailed stylized 3D environment with readable
   > props, dramatic motivated lighting ([warm amber lamps against deep blue night] / [golden-hour sun,
   > long blue shadows] / [bright saturated midday]), volumetric light rays, rim light, deep soft
   > shadows, shallow depth of field, rich saturated colours. No captions.
4. **Animate each keyframe** with image-to-video (Dreamina video, Kling, Veo/Flow), using one camera
   move per clip: "slow push-in on his face", "dolly through the window into the street", "fast whip
   tilt down to the holster with motion blur", "slow overhead slide across the desk".
5. **Voice:** ElevenLabs, read fast and energetic, about 220 words per minute, almost no pauses.
6. **Edit (I can do all of this):**
   - align the clips to your voice word by word
   - cross-dissolve between them, with a whip or zoom-through on action beats
   - burn in captions in exactly this style
   - add music and sound effects, and level everything to YouTube loudness

**Keep it original.** Copying his characters, stories or scripts would get your channel copyright
strikes and flagged as reused content. Use the formula and the look with your own characters and facts.
